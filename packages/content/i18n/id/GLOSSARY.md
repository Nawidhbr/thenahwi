# Indonesian glossary (id)

Use these renderings everywhere: in the translations of the books, the lessons, the terms, the tests and the
interface. The running text uses the technical words (column "in the text"), as the pesantren teaches ṣarf and naḥw
(the Amtsilah at-Tashrifiyyah, the Jurumiyah, the 'Imrithi, the Alfiyah). When the reader touches such a word, a pop-up
gives the plain word (column "pop-up"; a dash: no pop-up). The pop-ups are in `packages/content/glossary/id.json`;
keep that file in step with this list. The layout follows the English source list, `packages/content/i18n/GLOSSARY.md`.

## Style

- Plain, easy Bahasa Indonesia for santri and interested adults. Short sentences; one idea per sentence where
  possible. The author's sentences in their order; no paraphrase and no commentary in a translation.
- Technical terms are the Arabic terms in their usual pesantren spelling: ش = sy, ص = sh, ض = dh, ط = th, ظ = zh,
  ذ = dz, ث = ts, خ = kh, غ = gh, ʿayn and hamza = ' (straight apostrophe), tāʾ marbūṭa = -ah, long vowels unmarked
  in terms (fi'il madhi, fi'il mudhari', mashdar, isim fa'il, i'rab, marfu', manshub, majrur, majzum, mubtada',
  khabar, fa'il, maf'ul bih). No diacritics on terms in running text.
- **Arabic examples** inside a passage: in transliteration, then the meaning in quotation marks: *qama Zaidun*
  “Zaid telah berdiri”. The transliteration of examples follows the same letters as the terms, and like them leaves long
  vowels unmarked; it writes the endings (*Zaidun, Zaidan, Zaidin*), so the i'rab can be seen. The
  article is written as pronounced: *al-kitabu*, *ar-rajulu*, *bi-zaidin*; after a vowel it is joined (*fid-dari*,
  *bil-qaumi*, *qamal-qaumu*), and so is Allah (*bismillahi*, *lillahi*, *ilallahi*). The forms of the translations are
  made with this rule by script, so they stay the same in every book.
- No marks on Latin letters (the owner's rule, 2026-11-27): no â, î, û, neither in terms nor in examples (ila, 'ala,
  fi, qala Zaidun); the apostrophe for ʿayn and hamza stays. Where two forms would then look alike in one place, a
  short word in brackets tells them apart instead of a mark: a dual in a list without its meaning (nushira nushira
  [tatsniyah] nushiru), a form with an alif beside one without (if'alla [dengan alif], fa'ala [dengan alif],
  nushsharun [dengan alif] wa nushsharun), a ya' (fi'alan wa fi'alan [dengan ya']), a plural (nashiratun [jamak]), a
  wawu that drops (asalnya li-yaqul [dengan wawu]).
- Where the English gives an English word with its transliteration ("the doer (fāʿil)"), the Indonesian term is the
  Arabic word itself, so it is written once (“fa'il”); at the first mention in a definition a short Indonesian
  explanation may stand in brackets (“fa'il (pelaku)”). No Latin grammar words: do not use subjek, predikat, objek,
  nominatif, akusatif, genitif, jusif, kasus, infleksi, deklinasi, konjugasi (say tashrif), preposisi (say huruf
  jar), kata sifat (say na'at or shifat), kata benda, kata kerja, partikel (say isim, fi'il, huruf).
- Proper names and book titles in their usual Indonesian form: Zaid, 'Amr, Sibawaih, Ibnu Malik, Ibnu Ajurrum, Ibnu
  Hisyam, al-Jurjani, Alfiyah, Jurumiyah (al-Ajurrumiyyah in the title of the book), Qathrun Nada.
- Dates as in the English: “723/1323”; “d.” becomes “w.” (“(w. 723/1323)”).
- Quotation marks: “…”; a quotation inside a quotation: ‘…’. A heading of the book (bab, fashal) is translated:
  “Bab Kalam”, “Bab I'rab”, “Fashal”.
- **Reserved words**, which must not be used in their everyday sense, or the reader gets a wrong pop-up:
  - “kalimah” only for the word (kalimah: isim, fi'il, huruf); a word in general is “kata”;
  - “kalam” only for speech in the sense of the grammarians; ordinary speech is “ucapan”, “perkataan”;
  - “huruf” for a particle (harf) and for a letter of the alphabet, as the pesantren says; where the two must be
    kept apart, a particle is “huruf (harf ma'na)” and a letter “huruf hijaiyah”;
  - “bab” only for a chapter of the book and for a conjugation class of the verb (bab nashara); a door is “pintu”;
  - “khabar” only for the predicate; news is “berita”;
  - “hal” only for the ḥāl; “matter, thing” is “perkara”, “masalah” (never “hal” in its everyday sense);
  - “sifat” in everyday Indonesian means quality: for the adjective of the grammarians write “na'at” or “shifat”.

## Choices between competing terms

- khafḍ and jarr: the Jurumiyah says khafadh, and the translation of a book keeps the word of the book (khafadh,
  makhfudh; jar, majrur); in explanations “khafadh (jar)”.
- “mubtada'” is never rendered “subjek”, and “fa'il” never “subjek” either: the two terms stay.
- ma'lum and ma'ruf: the word of the book; pesantren lessons say “fi'il ma'lum” / “fi'il majhul”.
- mukhathab and hadhir: the word of the book (hadhir in the Ottoman and Persian primers).
- The augmented forms by their mashdar (bab if'al, bab taf'il, bab mufa'alah …), as in the Amtsilah at-Tashrifiyyah;
  the bare bābs by their example (bab nashara-yanshuru, bab dharaba-yadhribu …).
- Jamak, not “jama'”, in running text; “jama'” only inside a term already used so in the pesantren (jama' taksir is
  also heard: write jamak taksir).
- Indonesian “-nya” is joined to a term as usual (fa'ilnya, khabarnya).
- The reader is addressed as **Anda** everywhere (interface, lessons, tasks, tests; decided 2026-11-27): “kemajuan
  Anda”, never -mu or kamu, except inside the meaning of an Arabic example (أَبُوكَ “bapakmu”, نَصَرَنِي “dia telah
  menolongku”).
- Book names in running text and titles: al-Mabadi', Jurumiyah (title: al-Ajurrumiyyah), al-Amtsilah (Emsile), Bina'
  al-Af'al, al-Maqshud, al-'Izzi, Marah al-Arwah, al-'Awamil (Imam Birgivi), Izhar al-Asrar, al-'Awamil al-Mi'ah
  (al-Jurjani), Mizan ash-Sharf, Nahw-i Mir, Hidayatun Nahwi, al-Kafiyah, asy-Syafiyah, Syarh Jami (Mulla Jami),
  Qathrun Nada, Lamiyatul Af'al, Alfiyah. People: Ibnul Hajib, Ibnu 'Aqil, az-Zanjani, 'Abdul Qahir al-Jurjani, al-Jami,
  Radhiyuddin al-Astarabadzi, Imam Birgivi.
- Site words: a test is “tes”, the exam before a certificate “ujian”, the exercises of a lesson “tamrin”, a model answer
  “contoh jawaban”, the encyclopedia's glossary “Glosarium”, the study list of terms “Istilah”, transliteration
  “transliterasi”, Arabic grammar “tata bahasa Arab”.
- Patterns that look alike without marks are told apart by a bracketed word: fa'il [dengan alif] (فَاعِل) beside
  fa'il [dengan ya'] (فَعِيل), fa'ala [dengan alif] (فَاعَلَ) beside fa'ala (فَعَلَ).
- In meanings of verb forms, the past is “telah …” and the muḍāriʿ “sedang …” or plain (“dia menolong”), as the
  Amtsilah's translations do; a person is made clear in brackets where the Indonesian pronoun does not show it:
  “dia (seorang perempuan) menolong”, “kalian (laki-laki)”.
- More brackets met in al-'Izzi and the Bina: [pr.] for a feminine form beside its masculine twin (irmi [pr.] beside
  irmi, qi [pr.] beside qi), [hamzah] / ['ain] where only those letters differ (ra'a [hamzah] “melihat” beside
  ra'a ['ain] “menggembala”), [ajwaf] / [naqish] (itala [ajwaf], itala [naqish]); where a bracket would be clumsy the
  Arabic pattern stands in parentheses (fa'la (فَعْلَى), if'anla (افْعَنْلَى)). The two letters ح and ه are both
  written ha', so the Arabic letter follows: ha' (ح), ha' (ه).
- The radicals are fa' fi'il, 'ain fi'il and lam fi'il; plain “lam” is the particle لَمْ.
- The tense ḥāl (in the Mizan ash-Sharf, beside madhi and mustaqbal) is “al-hal (waktu sekarang)”, then “waktu
  sekarang”; “hal” stays reserved for ḥāl, and the everyday “matter” is “perkara”. Book names added in round 2:
  al-'Awamil al-Jadidah (the Birgivi 'Awamil, title), al-'Awamil al-Mi'ah, Mizan ash-Sharf; the author Mehmed Birgivi.
- Look-alikes met in the Mizan: [tatsniyah], [jamak] and [pr.] beside a twin form (la taf'ula [tatsniyah], fa'ilatun
  [jamak]); [dengan alif] / [dengan ya'] for mif'al [dengan alif] (مِفْعَالٌ) and mafa'ilu [dengan ya'] (مَفَاعِيلُ).
- The particle هَلْ, which looks like the reserved “hal” without marks, is always written “hal (هَلْ)” (Nahw-i Mir);
  likewise anna (أَنَّى) beside anna (أَنَّ). In the Nahw-i Mir: lam juhud (lām al-jaḥd), wawu sharf, fa' sababiyyah,
  kam istifhamiyyah / khabariyyah, isim tam, ibtida', the nine causes as 'adl, washf, ta'nits, 'alamiyyah, 'ujmah, jamak
  (muntahal jumu'), tarkib, wazan fi'il, alif dan nun tambahan; badal kull, ba'dh, isytimal, ghalath.
- Patterns in the Lamiyatul Af'al, where three look alike without marks: fa'il [dengan alif] (فَاعِل), fa'il [dengan
  ya'] (فَعِيل) and fa'il [pendek] (فَعِل); fu'ul [dengan wawu] (فُعُول) beside fu'ul (فُعُل); maf'ul [tanpa wawu]
  (مَفْعُل) beside maf'ul; fa'al [dengan alif] (فَعَال) beside fa'al (فَعَل), likewise fi'al, fu'al; fa'ala [dengan alif
  maqshurah] (فَعَلَى). Metre: fa'ilun [dengan alif] (فَاعِلُن) beside fa'ilun (فَعِلُن). The poem itself: Lamiyah.
- Qathrun Nada (round 3): the book is “Qathrun Nada” (in full Qathrun Nada wa Ballush Shada), its author Ibnu Hisyam
  al-Anshari, his commentary Syarh Qathrin Nada; the site's conjugation engine is “mesin tashrif”. Terms as in the
  book's passages: nun niswah, wawu jamaah, ya' mukhathabah, dhamir rafa' yang berharakat, huruf na'aitu, al-amtsilah
  al-khamsah, maqshur, manqush; further ma kaffah, anna yang diringankan, lam ibtida', dhamir fashl, dhamir sya'n,
  rabith, 'a'id, fi'il 'ilm / fi'il zhann, af'alul qulub, ilgha', ta'liq, isytighal, tanazu', i'timad, hikayatul hal,
  syabih bil-mudhaf, nakirah maqshudah / ghairu maqshudah, tarkhim, istighatsah (mustaghats), nudbah (mandub),
  istitsna' muttashil / munqathi' / mufarragh, mustatsna minhu, na'at sababi, qath' (na'at yang diputus), 'athaf bayan /
  'athaf nasaq, idhafah ma'nawiyyah / lafzhiyyah; ulama Bashrah / ulama Kufah, Bani Tamim, penduduk Hijaz.
- The particle lām (ل), where it could be read as لَمْ, is named by its kind: lam jar (ل), lam ta'lil, lam thalab,
  lam juhud, lam ibtida'. Look-alikes of the Qathrun Nada: hati [pr.], qama [tatsniyah] rajulani, ya ghulami [dengan
  ya'] beside ya ghulami, ya ghulama [dengan alif], ya abati [dengan ya'], ya Manshu [dengan wawu], adh-dhariba
  [tatsniyah] / adh-dharibu [jamak] Zaidin, allati [jamak] (اللَّاتِي); else the Arabic beside it: dhamir (ضَامِرٌ)
  “kurus”, idza (إِذَا) as the pause of idzan. Hamzat al-wasl is joined like the article (faqdhi, idzibtala,
  manistatha'a).
