# French glossary (fr)

Use these renderings everywhere: in the translations of the books, the lessons, the terms, the tests and the
interface. The readers are students of Arabic grammar in the Maghrib, West Africa, France and Belgium who study it the
traditional way (al-Âjurrûmiyya, Qatr al-nadâ, la Lâmiyya, al-'Izzî …). The running text uses the technical words
(column "in the text"); when the reader touches such a word, a pop-up gives the plain word (column "pop-up"; a dash:
no pop-up). The pop-ups will live in `packages/content/glossary/fr.json` (not written yet); keep it in step with this
list when it is made. The layout follows the English source list, `packages/content/i18n/GLOSSARY.md`.

## Style

- Clear standard French, for students and interested adults: short sentences, the author's order, no paraphrase and
  no commentary in a translation. The interface says "vous".
- **Transliteration** as French books of Arabic grammar write it: the English (ALA-LC) form without the dots, the long
  vowels with a circumflex, ʿayn and hamza as an apostrophe: nahw, sarf, i'râb, fâ'il, mubtada', maf'ûl, mudâri',
  mâdî, masdar, binâ', 'âmil, ma'mûl, tanwîn, hâl, tamyîz. Digraphs stay as in the English (sh, kh, dh, th, gh): khabar,
  shart, dhahaba. No other marks on Latin letters (no ā, ṣ, ḥ, ʿ, ʾ). The French accents (é è ê à ç ô ï) stay in
  French words. The article is written as in the English: al-fâ'il, al-mubtada', ism al-fâ'il, fî l-bayti.
- Two letter names fall together without the dots: ṭāʾ and tāʾ (« tâ' »), ḥāʾ and hāʾ (« hâ' »). Where the text
  speaks of the letter itself and both could be meant, the Arabic letter follows in brackets: « le tâ' [ط] »,
  « le hâ' [ح] », « le hâ' [ه] ». sâd / sîn, dâd / dâl and zâ' / zây stay apart by their names.
- **Arabic examples** inside a passage: in this transliteration with the case endings, then the meaning in French
  quotation marks: qâma Zaydun « Zayd s'est levé », ra'aytu Zaydan « j'ai vu Zayd », marartu bi-Zaydin « je suis passé
  près de Zayd ». The Arabic script is copied, never retyped.
- Where the English gives an English word with its transliteration ("the doer (fāʿil)"), the French gives the French
  word with the same transliteration: « l'agent (fâ'il) »; after that the transliterated term alone, as in the English.
  Where the English uses only the transliteration, so does the French.
- No Western grammar words for the Arab terms: not "nominatif, accusatif, génitif, jussif" (say raf', nasb, jarr /
  khafd, jazm), not "complément du nom" for the idâfa, not "accompli / inaccompli" for mâdî / mudâri'.
- Proper names and book titles as French Islamic studies write them: Sîbawayh, al-Khalîl, Ibn Mâlik, Ibn Âjurrûm,
  Ibn Hishâm, Ibn al-Hâjib, al-Zamakhsharî, al-Suyûtî, 'Abd al-Qâhir al-Jurjânî, al-Sayyid al-Sharîf al-Jurjânî,
  al-Birgivî, al-Taftâzânî, al-Zanjânî; al-Âjurrûmiyya, l'Alfiyya, Qatr al-nadâ, la Lâmiyyat al-af'âl, al-Kâfiya,
  al-Shâfiya. The examples' people: Zayd, 'Amr, Bakr, Khâlid. The French article comes before a title only where
  French needs it ("la Lâmiyya", "l'Alfiyya"; "al-Âjurrûmiyya" keeps its own).
- Dates as in the English: « 723/1323 »; « d. » becomes « m. » (« (m. 723/1323) »).
- Punctuation in the French way: a space before « : ; ! ? », quotation marks « … » with spaces, inside them “…”.
  A heading of a book is translated: « Chapitre du discours », « Chapitre de l'i'râb », « Section ».
- **Reserved words**, which must not be used in their everyday sense, or the reader gets a wrong pop-up:
  - « mot » only for the kalima; a word in the everyday sense is « terme » or « vocable »;
  - « discours » only for the kalâm of the grammarians;
  - « particule » only for the harf that brings a meaning; a letter of the alphabet is « lettre »;
  - « sujet » only for the mubtada'; the fâ'il is « l'agent », never « sujet »;
  - « prédicat » only for the khabar; « classe » only for the bâb of the verbs; « schème » only for the wazn;
  - « état » only for the four states of i'râb (l'état de raf' …); the hâl is « le hâl (complément d'état) ».

## Choices between competing terms

- khafḍ and jarr: the translation of a book keeps the word of the book (khafd, makhfûd in al-Âjurrûmiyya; jarr,
  majrûr in Qatr al-nadâ and most later books); in explanations « le khafd (jarr) ».
- « sujet » is the mubtada', never the fâ'il (« l'agent »); « prédicat » is the khabar.
- « tawkîd » of the followers is « la corroboration »; the nûn al-tawkîd of the verb is « le nûn d'insistance ».
- « badal » is « le permutatif », so that it is not confused with « le substitut de l'agent » (nâ'ib al-fâ'il).
- ma'lûm and ma'rûf, mukhâtab and hâdir: the word of the book; in French « actif », « deuxième personne » for both.
- The augmented forms by their masdar (la forme if'âl, la forme taf'îl, mufâ'ala …), never by Western numbers in a
  translation (a lesson may add « forme IV »); the bâbs of the bare verb by their example (la classe de nasara).
- The verbs of the tables: by their place in the paradigm (troisième personne du masculin singulier first).
