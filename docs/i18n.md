# Languages and translation

English is the source language and the main audience. Everything is built and written in English first; the
translations follow in a separate pass, so new pages may run on the English fallback for a while. All translations
are drafts for review, like the English.

## The languages

`SITE.languages` lists nine: en, nl, ar, ur, fa, tr, id, ru, fr (kept from The Mantiqi). A language goes live
(`LIVE_LOCALES`) only when its content is complete and it has an interface file (`src/i18n/<code>.json`); live ones
link to the same page in that language from the gear menu, the rest show "In preparation". Only English is live: the
other dictionaries and content translations of The Mantiqi were left out of the copy, since none of their words is
about ṣarf or naḥw. The first language to follow is the owner's choice (Urdu, Indonesian and Turkish have the most
madrasa readers of these books). ar, ur and fa are right to left (`dirOf`). Until a language is live, the tests that
need it are skipped (`e2e/flags.ts`).

## Where translations live

- **Interface**: `apps/web/src/i18n/<code>.json`, the same keys as `en.json`, merged over it. Use `t(locale)` and
  `fmt()`; never write interface text into a page.
- **Course content**, per language in `packages/content/i18n/<code>/`:
  - `terms/<slug>.json`, `quizzes/<slug>.json`, `matn.json` (not for `ar`), `exercises/<chapter>.json`;
  - the engine's generated questions (`packages/engine/src/exercises.ts`) are built from Arabic forms and short English
    prompts in the UI dictionary (`practice`), so they need only the interface translated;
  - `primers/<id>.json` (schema `PrimerTranslation`, merged by `primerIn`; no `text` for a passage in the reader's own
    language, none at all in Arabic; a translated passage carries `lang` and is shown in the reader's language);
  - `commentaries/<id>/quizzes/<n>.json` for the tests of the books read lesson by lesson.
- **Books read lesson by lesson**: the blocks in `commentaries/<id>/<code>.json`; lesson and part titles as a `<code>`
  key in `lessons.json` and `outline.json`.
- **Encyclopedia**: `i18n/<code>/background/people/<slug>.json` and `background/articles/<slug>.json`, the same set
  of all 100 scholars and 14 articles in every language once a language is done (the counts of 27 November 2026). The glossary's concepts go in
  `i18n/<code>/background/concepts/<slug>.json`; until a concept is translated its page shows the English and points
  its canonical at `/en/`.
- **Word pop-ups**: `packages/content/glossary/<code>.json` (`docs/study.md`).

`npm run validate -w @nahwi/content` checks counts and ids and writes `generated/i18n.json` and the other bundles.

## Terminology

`packages/content/i18n/GLOSSARY.md` is the English source list: every concept (English | Arabic) under eleven
chapter headings of al-Mabādiʾ, the English style, and the layout every language follows. Each language has a `GLOSSARY.md` in
`packages/content/i18n/<code>/` in that layout (Style; Words under the same headings; Choices between competing
terms) that fixes its terms: use it for every translation and keep `glossary/<code>.json` in step
with it. A concept added to the source list gets a row in every language. A choice not in the glossary goes into it
once made. Rules shared by all:

- the running text keeps the established technical terms; the plain words live in the pop-ups;
- a pop-up form matches its word anywhere, so a technical word is never used in its everyday sense (the reserved
  words in each glossary's Style);
- no transliterations in brackets in running text, except where the text discusses the Arabic word itself;
- the same concept always gets the same word, in every book;
- names of people and books as in the English (Ibn Mālik, the Alfiyya), with the language's own articles;
- the Arabic examples and every conjugated form stay Arabic, vocalised, in every language.

## Pages that are not translated

A knowledge-base page in a language counts as its own once its text is translated (`lib/translated.ts`, tested in
`test/translated.test.ts`): a scholar page or article when its translation exists, and a page that gathers them (the
home page, the scholars, the two sections, a handbook) when every scholar and article it gathers is translated. A
library work counts once its entry in `i18n/<code>/background/library.json` exists (the summary, and a title or full
title only where the English one has English words in it: in ar, ur and fa these stay in Latin transliteration), and
`/books/` once every work and handbook does; the bundle is `generated/library-i18n.json` (`getLibrary(locale)`). The
rest (the catalogues, the commentaries' editions pages, the news) stays readable in English but puts its canonical on
the English page, carries no hreflang and is left out of the sitemap.

## Right to left

Use logical CSS properties (`margin-inline-start`, `inset-inline-end`), not left and right. Diagrams stay left to
right; labels in boxes carry `data-fit` and shrink if a translation runs long.

## Latin letters without marks (since 27 November 2026)

Content is written with the scholarly transliteration (ALA-LC in English: ṣarf, naḥw, iʿrāb), but the site serves
every page and data file through `apps/web/src/middleware.ts`, which calls `lib/plain-latin.ts`: Latin letters lose
the marks that the page's language does not have (sarf, nahw, i'rab), ʿayn and hamza become an apostrophe, and the
letters of the page's own language stay (Turkish ç ş ğ ı ö ü, French é è ê à ç …, Dutch ë ï). Arabic script is never
touched. Scripts that write text of their own do the same (`scripts/parent-news.js` for The Mantiqi's feed,
`scripts/make-og.cjs` for the share images). The Turkish and Indonesian translations are also written without â, î,
û (their `GLOSSARY.md`). Keep writing content in the scholarly form: the plain form is made when serving.
